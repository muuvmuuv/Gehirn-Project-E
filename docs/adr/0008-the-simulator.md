# ADR-0008: The simulator

**Status:** Accepted on 2026-10-06; the armor widens its keeps by the base's stopping distance (PLAN, Known issue 33)
**Date:** 2026-10-06
**Deciders:** repository owner

## Context

Phase 3 wants a better body than `body.Sim`, a planar point mass that slides sideways, integrates on every `sense` by the wall clock and lets the walking human pass through it. Its Task 3 asks for this ADR: MuJoCo through its C API, or Gazebo through ROS 2 and rmw_zenoh. Task 1 brings worlds as files, with humans that loop, walk waypoints, stand or walk toward the body, and stop or step aside; Task 2 gives `Sim` a differential drive whose adapter the armor applies; Task 4 then builds a `Body` with a differential drive base on the chosen simulator, while the stack above stays holonomic and `Sim` stays for fast runs; and Task 5 replaces ground truth obstacles with a range sensor. The owner installed MuJoCo 3.14.0 on the Mac through Homebrew's `mujoco` cask, and `just mujoco` links it into `thirdparty/mujoco`.

Five facts shape the decision, measured on 2026-10-06 unless they say otherwise.

1. Invariant 9 reaches the body. The armor holds the body and only the 50 Hz field loop calls it (Invariant 1), so the body is field tier code, which may link a C library only if it builds for aarch64 musl. A simulator linked into the field unit has to build there, or run as a process apart from the field unit, where hardware will stand.
2. MuJoCo 3.14.0 builds for aarch64 musl. In an alpine:3.22 container (Alpine 3.22.6, GCC 14.2.0, CMake 3.31.7, musl 1.2.5, aarch64, in Docker's VM with 8 CPUs and 7.7 GiB on an M3 Pro), the source tarball of tag 3.14.0 built the shared library upstream ships once C files got `-D_POSIX_C_SOURCE=200809L`. Without it the build stops at an `#error` in `src/engine/engine_util_errmem.c`, which looks for `_POSIX_C_SOURCE` before calling `localtime_r`: glibc defines that macro under the `_GNU_SOURCE` that MuJoCo's CMake sets, and musl does not, though it declares `localtime_r`. Upstream hardcodes `add_library(mujoco SHARED ...)`, so a static library takes a patch of two lines to `CMakeLists.txt` (Distribution): that line made `STATIC`, and the install rules dropped, since CMake refuses to export a static `mujoco` whose fetched dependencies sit in no export set. At `-j4` the shared library and its plugins built in 26 s after a configure of 6 s, and the static library alone in 29 s after 8 s. `libmujoco.so.3.14.0` is 4.8 MB; `libmujoco.a` is 8.6 MB, plus 1.6 MB for the archives of libccd, qhull, tinyobjloader, tinyxml2 and miniz. The same tarball, flag and patch built the static library on Ubuntu 24.04 (glibc 2.39, GCC 13.3.0, aarch64) in 19 s after 6 s, and on the Mac (Apple clang 21, CMake 4.4.4) in 13 s after 11 s. CMake fetches seven dependencies over git at the commits 3.14.0 names: lodepng, MarchingCubeCpp, qhull, tinyxml2, tinyobjloader, libccd and miniz.
3. V drives it. Scratch V programs with the `#flag` lines of PLAN's Phase 3 Task 3, built with `v -W`, ran on the Mac against the framework of 3.14.0's macOS image (fact 5), with an rpath to it, and against the static library built there, with its archives and libc++ added to the link, and as C that V generated on the Mac with `-os linux -gc none`, linked by gcc against the shared and the static library on musl and the static one on glibc. Every build printed the same values, and only the sign of zero differed: the base at rest reads `vx -0.000 m/s` on the Mac and `vx 0.000 m/s` on Linux. The static binaries are 7.2 MB on musl, 6.9 MB on glibc and 4.1 MB on the Mac, where only libSystem and libc++ stay dynamic. The first program parses MJCF from a string with `mj_parseXMLString`, compiles it with `mj_compile` and steps it with `mj_step`: a ball dropped from 1 m rests at z 0.100 m after 500 steps of 2 ms, and a 20 kg planar base on slide and hinge joints with velocity actuators, commanded at 1 m/s toward the pillar of `Sim`'s scene, moves at 1.000 m/s after 1 s, touches the pillar at 1.020 s and stands at x -1.050 m, the pillar's rim less the base's 0.25 m radius. The second installs a log handler from V with `mju_setLogHandler`, which then receives every message, the compile time at the info level included, and ends the process with one line on `mju_error`; MuJoCo writes no `MUJOCO_LOG.TXT` then. Its model compiles with `<compiler usethread="false"/>` and sets `<flag autoreset="disable"/>`, which it reads back; it tells contacts apart by geom, and walks a mocap human past the standing base, 0.15 m off its center, which pushes the base 0.40 m aside and 0.80 m along, and then through the pillar without a contact. After an infinite velocity and 5 steps with autoreset off, the state is NaN and the warning counters read 8 for `mjWARN_BADQPOS` and 10 each for `mjWARN_BADQVEL` and `mjWARN_BADQACC`, each reported to the handler once. A third, on the Mac only, turns the base to face +y and drives its x slide: it moves along the world's x, so the slides keep the world's axes whatever the hinge's angle. Ten steps, one field tick of 20 ms, took a mean of 24 to 60 µs per run on the Mac and 27 to 30 µs in the containers, at most 185 µs for one tick, with the host's load average between 2.2 and 5.2. The Mac's upper end comes from the host's load more than from the static library: its runs of 43 to 60 µs came right after its build, at a load of 4.45, and run in turn with the other two Mac builds at a load of 2.0 it took 29 µs against their 26 to 27 µs.
4. Neither Gazebo nor ROS 2 Lyrical lists a musl platform. [ROS 2 Lyrical Luth](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ros2/ros2_documentation/rolling/source/Releases/lyrical/supported-platforms.rst), the LTS since 2026-05-22, is Tier 1 on Ubuntu 26.04 for amd64 and arm64 and on Windows 11, Tier 3 on macOS for amd64 only, and lists no Alpine or musl platform; rmw_zenoh_cpp is a Tier 1 middleware there, though not the default. The community's [alpine-ros](https://github.com/alpine-ros/alpine-ros) images bring ROS 2 Jazzy and Humble to Alpine 3.23, but not Gazebo. [Gazebo Jetty](https://gazebosim.org/docs/jetty/install/), the LTS that pairs with Lyrical, is supported officially on Ubuntu Noble amd64 and at best effort on arm, Windows and macOS, whose binaries come from the osrf/simulation Homebrew tap for [Ventura and Sonoma](https://gazebosim.org/docs/jetty/install_osx/). [rmw_zenoh](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ros2/rmw_zenoh/lyrical/docs/design.md) speaks a design of its own on top of Zenoh: a key expression per topic with the type's hash, CDR payloads, an attachment with a sequence number, a timestamp and the publisher's id, liveliness tokens for discovery, and a router, `rmw_zenohd`. A plain Zenoh application has to copy all of it, which its [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ros2/rmw_zenoh/lyrical/README.md) calls beyond the repository's goals. Its Lyrical branch vendors zenoh-c 1.10.1 with a few fixes, the release gehirn pins.
5. The cask moves with upstream. MuJoCo released 3.12.0 on 2026-08-20, 3.13.0 on 2026-09-09, 3.14.0 on 2026-09-22 and 3.15.0 on 2026-10-05, and Homebrew's cask installs each release's macOS image. On 2026-10-06 at 08:31 Homebrew upgraded the owner's cask from 3.14.0 to 3.15.0, so `/Applications/MuJoCo.app` no longer holds the library `just mujoco` links: the recipe refuses, and the link in `thirdparty/mujoco` points at a file that is gone. The Mac probes of fact 3 therefore linked 3.14.0 from the release's macOS image, checked against the release's sha256 file. The first probe printed the same against the cask's 3.15.0.

## Decision

**MuJoCo in process.** The field unit links MuJoCo 3.14.0 through its C API, on the field loop's thread, behind a new module `mujoco` that is the only door to it, as `zenoh` is to zenoh-c. `mujoco` loads a model from MJCF text, steps it, and reads and writes the few arrays a body needs, such as positions, velocities, controls, mocap positions, contacts and warnings; it knows nothing of LCL. A `Body` in `body` builds the model from the world and implements the robot API on it.

**Only with `-d mujoco`.** Every file that touches MuJoCo ends in `_d_mujoco.v` or `_d_mujoco.c.v`, which V 0.5.2 compiles only under `-d mujoco` (`vlib/v/pref/should_compile.v`). The default build, `just check`, the hooks and the workflows of `.github/workflows` compile none of it and need no MuJoCo; a CI job of its own builds MuJoCo and the `-d mujoco` tests. Tests are the exception to the file rule: `v test .` compiles a `_d_mujoco_test.v` file without the define and fails, so a test that needs MuJoCo sits in a `$if mujoco ? {}` block of an ordinary `_test.v` file, which passes both ways, as a scratch module showed.

**Chosen at runtime.** `BODY` picks the body of the field unit and of the combined binary: `sim`, the default, or `mujoco`. A build without `-d mujoco` refuses `BODY=mujoco` at startup and names the variable and the define (CONTRIBUTING, Configuration 2). Both bodies read the world that `WORLD` names. The MuJoCo body is always a differential one, whichever drive Task 2's switch picks for `Sim`.

**The base.** A planar body on three joints, a slide along x, a slide along y and a hinge about z, in that order, so the slides move it along the world's axes whatever its heading. Each joint has a velocity actuator with a force limit, and the body a cylinder of 0.25 m radius, the reach of `Sim`'s contact check. No gravity acts on a planar joint, and the floor sits below the base so they never touch. The stack above stays holonomic and 2D: the percept carries the planar pose, velocity and heading, and the bridge draws a plane. The armor steers, as Task 2 builds it for `Sim`: `Armor.drive` restrains the planar command as today, turns it into a `body.Course` through the shared adapter `body.steer`, checks the motion that course makes along the body's heading against every restraint again, the speed, the fence, `human_stop` and `solid_keep`, and actuates `[speed, heading]`, or a turn in place where that motion breaks one. The Body sets the slides' controls to that speed along the heading of the percept the armor checked, `speed cos θ` and `speed sin θ`, held until the next `actuate`, and the hinge's to `body.turn_rate` toward the commanded heading. So the base turns before it drives and is commanded only where the armor looked; the actuators blend the previous checked motion into the next with a time constant of mass over gain, 0.05 s for 20 kg at a gain of 400, as a real base's inertia does, so once the armor takes a motion out, the base brakes along it over its stopping distance, 6.25 cm from 1 m/s, which the armor does not check (PLAN, Known issue 33). This gives up the wheels: slip and skid, a caster's drag, bumps and tipping. The force limit keeps the motors' saturation. Wheels on a floor, as MuJoCo's own `model/car/car.xml` builds them, with a free joint chassis, two hinged wheels, a frictionless sphere for a caster and tendons for forward and turn, wait until Open question 1 names the first real base, whose wheels and motors they would copy.

**The world.** The Body writes the MJCF text from the world `WORLD` names, so one file describes the scene for both bodies and no MJCF file can drift from it. An obstacle becomes a static geom of its shape and radius. A human becomes a mocap body with a capsule of its radius, which the Body moves before every step as `Sim` moves the same human, by its walk and its reaction to the body from Task 1's world file, at the model's clock and with the base's pose, so a human who stops or steps aside does so in both bodies. MuJoCo holds a mocap body fixed in its dynamics and gives it no contacts with static geoms or with other mocap bodies (`doc/XMLreference.rst`, body mocap, and `doc/computation/index.rst`), so a human goes where those rules put it, through a pillar or another human included. Its capsule takes part in no contact with the base either. A mocap body pushes with whatever force its walk takes, and a human that walked through a base standing beside a pillar squeezed the base into the pillar and flung it out at about 8 m/s, so the Body tells a human touching the base by `Sim`'s rule and holds the base still while they touch, as `Sim` does, where the second probe showed a human pushing the base aside. A beacon is no geom, since nothing touches it. The percept's `contact` is any contact of the base's geom, or a human touching it.

**Stepping and timing.** MuJoCo steps at 2 ms. Each `sense` steps the model until its clock reaches the wall clock time since the Body was made, less the time it has dropped, at most 25 steps, 50 ms, per call, and drops whatever lies beyond that. A stall of the field loop thus becomes a pause, as `main.v` `pace` starts again from now rather than run ticks back to back: the model never runs ahead of the wall clock or faster than it, and the humans' mocap bodies, which follow the model's clock, pause with it. The percept's `t_ms` stays the field unit's clock, so the recorder and the tools read it as they do today. Like `Sim` and a real motor controller, the Body zeroes its controls before it steps once the last `actuate` is 200 ms old, so the base brakes on its own when commands stop. Autoreset is off (`<flag autoreset="disable"/>`), so MuJoCo never puts the body back at its start on its own after a bad step (`src/engine/engine_forward.c` `mj_checkPos`, `mj_checkVel`, `mj_checkAcc`).

**Threads, logs and errors.** Only the field loop calls the Body, through the armor, so the model and its data see one thread. The MJCF the Body writes sets `<compiler usethread="false"/>`, so compiling the model starts no thread either, and names no plugin, which a static binary cannot load (Distribution). The Body starts no MuJoCo thread pool and sets no `mjcb_` callback. Its one callback is a log handler that `mujoco` installs with `mju_setLogHandler`, which runs on the thread that called MuJoCo, the field loop's, so no V code runs on a thread MuJoCo started (ADR-0003, fact 4). It replaces MuJoCo's default handler, which appends every warning and error to `MUJOCO_LOG.TXT` in the working directory and prints it to stderr without a source (`src/engine/engine_util_errmem.c`). The handler ignores info and debug messages, which MuJoCo hands a handler unfiltered, and warnings, which the step reads from `mjData.warning`. On an error it prints MuJoCo's message as one line that starts with `field: mujoco:` and ends the process, since MuJoCo's error path must not return. After every step `mujoco` reads the warning counters, and once any of them rises, an unstable state (`mjWARN_BADQPOS`, `mjWARN_BADQVEL`, `mjWARN_BADQACC`) or a full contact or constraint buffer among them, it raises that as an error through `mju_error`, before `sense` builds a percept. So every failure inside MuJoCo while running ends the field unit with one line and stops the simulated body with it, the outcome ADR-0001 asks of a stalled field process, and no NaN reaches a percept, which `x.json2` would write as `nan`, a text no JSON reader decodes. This bends two rules of CONTRIBUTING, each for a reason the rule's own words admit. Logging 1 lets a thread with nothing to return to log its own failure once and end, and a log handler has nothing to return to. Errors 2 keeps `panic` to startup, but MuJoCo's error path ends the process whatever gehirn does, and a model that has gone unstable is a fault in the model that no halt repairs. A model that fails to parse or compile returns MuJoCo's message instead, and gehirn refuses to start with it.

**Distribution.** One static library from MuJoCo 3.14.0's source on every platform, the Mac included. `just mujoco` fetches `https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/archive/refs/tags/3.14.0.tar.gz`, checks it against sha256 `4e1ebd14406967f4554a9c6ed613fe7f88a8ea8a9c272ede80d083a25f9e31a9`, as `scripts/zenoh.sh` checks zenoh-c, applies this patch to `CMakeLists.txt`:

builds with:

```sh

    -DCMAKE_C_FLAGS=-D_POSIX_C_SOURCE=200809L -DBUILD_SHARED_LIBS=OFF -DMUJOCO_ENABLE_LTO=OFF \

```

and puts the result into `thirdparty/mujoco` in the layout the Mac's link uses today, `include/mujoco` for the headers and `lib` for the archives. Link time optimization stays off so the archive holds plain objects rather than the compiler's bytecode. `--target mujoco` builds none of MuJoCo's plugin libraries, elasticity, actuator, sensor and sdf, which the base, its geoms, mocap bodies and rangefinders do not need; the OBJ and STL decoders compile into `libmujoco.a`. A binary then links `libmujoco.a`, `libccd.a`, `libqhullstatic_r.a`, `libtinyobjloader.a`, `libtinyxml2.a` and `libminiz.a` from `build/lib`, with `-lstdc++ -lm -lpthread` on Linux and `-lc++` on the Mac, and the binding names the archives in `#flag` lines as `zenoh/zenoh.c.v` names `libzenohc.a`. Plugins stay out of reach: on glibc the static link warns that `mj_loadPluginLibrary`'s `dlopen` needs glibc's shared libraries at run time, and a static musl binary carries only a stub of `dlopen`. The configure step fetches the seven dependencies over git at pinned commits, so the first build needs cmake, a C++ compiler, git and the network, which only a `-d mujoco` build asks for. The release uploads no source archive, so the pin is on GitHub's generated one, whose contents GitHub keeps stable but whose compression it may change with six months' notice: a changed archive fails the check and stops the build, and the fix is a new checksum once its contents match the tag's commit `9ecbb9d7b5ee623f54745638d36799ff90e6f7cd`, which a shallow git fetch of that commit would pin without the archive.

The Mac leaves the cask. A pin that a Homebrew upgrade moves is no pin, as fact 5 showed on the day of the install, and one source on every platform keeps the Mac on the version every Linux build runs. The static library also leaves every binary self contained, with no rpath into `/Applications`, as the static `libzenohc.a` does. Two other ways were weighed. The release's macOS image of 3.14.0, checked by its sha256 as the probes took it, would spare the Mac the compiler, but its framework is a dylib that every binary has to find through an rpath, as the cask's is. Upstream's prebuilt Linux tarballs, for aarch64 and x86_64 with sha256 files beside them, would spare glibc hosts the compiler and the network, but they are glibc only and hold a shared `libmujoco.so` that needs an rpath too, while musl needs the source build anyway. Moving the pin is its own commit that runs the probes' cases again, as for zenoh-c; 3.15.0 is out, and the first probe printed the same against it.

**Licensing.** MuJoCo is under the Apache License 2.0. Its fetched dependencies are permissive: libccd under BSD, lodepng and tinyxml2 under zlib, miniz and tinyobjloader under MIT, MarchingCubeCpp under MIT or the public domain, as its README offers, and qhull under its own license. None is copyleft, gehirn stays under the EUPL 1.2, and a binary built with `-d mujoco` carries their notices.

## Options Considered

### Option A: MuJoCo in process, behind `-d mujoco`

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Invariant 9 | Holds: MuJoCo builds for aarch64 musl with one flag, static with a patch of two lines, and both probes ran as static musl binaries |
| New processes | None |
| New code | The `mujoco` module, one `Body`, and the world written as MJCF |
| Default build and CI | Unchanged: nothing compiles MuJoCo without the define; CI gains a `-d mujoco` job of its own |
| Cost per field tick | A mean of 24 to 60 µs on the Mac and 27 to 30 µs in Linux containers, of a 20 ms tick |

**Pros:** the body stays a call away from the field loop with no new link, key or clock, so `tools/trials.py`, the demo and the scenes fly it with one variable. The calls the design rests on ran from V on the Mac, on musl and on glibc: parsing, compiling and stepping a model, velocity actuators, a mocap human that pushes the base and passes through a pillar, contacts told apart by geom, the compiler's thread switch, the autoreset flag, the warning counters and a log handler that ends the process on an error. MuJoCo's `rangefinder` sensors and `mj_multiRay` would give Task 5 its range sensor without a renderer, though no probe has cast a ray yet.
**Cons:** a field binary built with the define carries MuJoCo, its dependencies and the C++ runtime: the static probe was 7.2 MB on musl. An error inside MuJoCo, or a model gone unstable, ends the field process. Every platform builds MuJoCo from source, with cmake, a C++ compiler and the network once, where zenoh-c comes prebuilt for every platform, and the patch is ours to carry until upstream builds a static library.

### Option B: MuJoCo in a simulator process apart from the field unit

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Invariant 9 | Holds without a musl build: the simulator stands where hardware will |
| New processes | One per field unit, started before it |
| New code | A body protocol, sealed under a key of its own, both of its ends, and a `Body` that sends commands and keeps the newest state |
| Default build and CI | Unchanged |
| Cost per field tick | A step plus a network hop each way |

**Pros:** the field unit reaches the simulator as it will reach a motor controller or a robot base, over a link, so a crash in the simulator leaves the field unit running, and the simulator may run any engine on any platform, glibc or macOS, from upstream's prebuilt libraries.
**Cons:** a body link carries actuation, so it needs its own key and the checks of ADR-0003, or anyone on the network steers the body. The field loop never waits on the network (CONTRIBUTING, Concurrency 4), so `sense` returns the newest state the link delivered, a tick or more old, and a lost link has to halt the body as a watchdog does. Every run of trials, the demo and the scenes starts and stops one more process. The link would rehearse a protocol that Phase 5 designs for the motor controller, and the probes show Invariant 9 does not force it.

### Option C: Gazebo through ROS 2 and rmw_zenoh

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Invariant 9 | Only in option B's shape, since neither ROS 2 Lyrical nor Gazebo lists Alpine or musl, and the community's ROS 2 for Alpine carries no Gazebo |
| Platforms | Gazebo Jetty officially on Ubuntu Noble amd64, at best effort on arm and macOS; ROS 2 Lyrical Tier 3 on macOS for amd64 only |
| Weight | `ros:lyrical-ros-base` 329 MB compressed for arm64; `osrf/ros:lyrical-simulation` 899 MB and `osrf/ros:lyrical-desktop-full` 1.9 GB, for amd64 only |
| Wire | rmw_zenoh's key expressions, CDR, attachments and liveliness tokens, through a router |
| Base and people | The [DiffDrive](https://gazebosim.org/docs/jetty/moving_robot/) system on `cmd_vel`; [actors](https://gazebosim.org/docs/jetty/actors/) on waypoint paths that take part in no physics |

**Pros:** the standard stack of robotics, with a differential drive, lidar and maps ready made, and a body on ROS 2 would reach many real bases' drivers unchanged, a path the README names for later. rmw_zenoh vendors the zenoh-c gehirn pins, and Gazebo Jetty can move its own transport onto Zenoh with `GZ_TRANSPORT_IMPLEMENTATION=zenoh` ([release notes](https://gazebosim.org/docs/latest/release_notes/)).
**Cons:** it takes option B's process split and adds ROS 2's. The field unit would speak rmw_zenoh's design in V, CDR included, or go through a ROS node that translates. ROS 2, Gazebo and a router run on an Ubuntu machine or container, since the Mac is a best effort platform for both, and the owner's macOS 27 is newer than the Ventura and Sonoma that Jetty's binaries name. The simulation image weighs about 0.9 GB compressed and exists for amd64 only. Actors feel no force and collide with nothing, so a human neither pushes the body nor touches it through the physics.

## Trade-off Analysis

The question Invariant 9 put was whether MuJoCo builds for aarch64 musl, and it does: one flag for the shared library, two lines of CMake for the static one, and both probes ran as static musl binaries. With that settled, the process split of options B and C buys isolation that a stand-in body does not need, and costs a link, a key, a state a tick old and one more process in every run. That link belongs to hardware, and Phase 5 designs it for the motor controller, whose firmware repeats the armor's limits; a simulator link built now would rehearse a protocol that hardware replaces. Gazebo adds a platform the project does not run, Ubuntu with ROS 2, about a gigabyte of install and a wire format gehirn would have to implement, for cameras, lidar and maps that Phase 3 does not need yet: Task 5's range sensor is a ring of rays, which MuJoCo casts itself. MuJoCo in process keeps gehirn one binary with one more variable, and `-d mujoco` keeps every check free of it.

The base on planar joints is the simpler model, and the probes showed it does what the stack needs. The velocity actuators follow the command with a lag of mass over gain, and a force limit of 200 N caps the push while the base speeds up, so after 1 s at 1 m/s the probe's base trailed an ideal point by 0.061 m: 0.048 m from the lag, as the same base without the limit trailed, and 0.013 m from the limit. Contact stopped it at the pillar's rim, and a mocap human pushed it aside. Nothing about wheels needs tuning before a real base names them. With the armor steering through Task 2's adapter, the MuJoCo body adds no direction of its own, so the restraints that hold `Sim` hold its commands too, while its inertia carries it along a motion the armor has just taken out by its stopping distance.

The cost lands on the field binary built with the define: megabytes of MuJoCo and the C++ runtime, a field unit that ends with MuJoCo's error or an unstable model, a source build on every platform and a patch to carry. Option B stays open: should a field computer prefer the simulator elsewhere, the same `mujoco` module can drive it from a process of its own.

## Consequences

Easier: Task 4 builds on a module and a Body whose calls the probes ran on three platforms. Trials, the demo and the scenes fly the MuJoCo body with `BODY=mujoco`. Obstacles that stop the body and the inertia of a base come from the physics rather than from `Sim`'s rules, and Task 5 casts its rays in the same model. Every platform runs the same pinned MuJoCo, and a `-d mujoco` binary needs no MuJoCo installed beside it.

Harder: a second build of gehirn, with `-d mujoco`, that `just check` never compiles, so Task 4 adds a recipe that builds and tests it and a CI job of its own that runs it.
