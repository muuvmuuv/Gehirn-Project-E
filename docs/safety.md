# Safety

The armor owns the body, and `main` never gets a handle to it. Every command passes a speed cap (1.0 m/s manned, 0.4 unmanned), an acceleration limit that never limits braking, a geofence, and speed and separation monitoring: slower from 2 m to a human, and inside 0.7 m nothing moves toward them. Nothing pushes into anything solid either; what remains of a command slides along the surface. Irreversible effectors need all three MAGI and no human within 2 m.

That is a soft layer on operating systems without real time guarantees. On hardware the same limits run again on the motor controller behind a physical e-stop, and the controller zeroes its outputs when commands go stale, as the simulator does after 200 ms. The reasoning is in [ADR 0001](adr/0001-where-it-runs.md).

When control fails, the stack falls to the safe state. A MAGI unit that errs, times out or answers nonsense votes no ([MAGI](magi.md#how-a-vote-works)). Without HQ there is no quorum, so nothing irreversible happens while the umbilical is cut: once HQ has been silent for `UMBILICAL_GRACE_MS` the unit runs on internal power for `INTERNAL_BUDGET_MS`, 5:00 by default, then the body stands still, whoever sits in the seat, until HQ's pulse reconnects the cable. The pilot's eject halts the body until the process restarts ([Piloting](piloting.md#datagrams)). These follow from [PLAN's Invariants](../PLAN.md#invariants), the full list every change keeps.

## How the body moves

The stack above the armor steers in planar velocities: the core's reflex, the pilot, the dummy plug and the armor's restraints all work on an `x,y` velocity. `DRIVE` decides what the simulated body makes of the restrained command. `holonomic`, the default, slides along it in any direction. `differential` is a base on two wheels that faces east at the start, the zero heading of a planar robot and 35 degrees off the beacon from `START`'s default, turns in place at up to 2 rad/s, about 115 degrees a second, and drives only along its heading, so it never slides sideways. `body.steer` maps the command onto it, turn then drive: the base turns toward the command and drives only while its heading lies within 30 degrees of it, at the command's speed projected onto the heading.

`PLANNER` decides what makes the core's command. `reflex`, the default, pulls the body toward the approved goal and pushes it off whatever is near. `local` is the local planner, built for the holonomic body. It finds the shortest way to the goal around every solid and every human who stands, each widened by the armor's keep plus a margin, 0.5 m from a solid's rim and 1 m from a standing human's, over the corners of a polygon drawn around each, and leaves out the corners within 0.15 m of the fence. Each tick it tries 32 headings at a third, two thirds and all of the speed the armor allows, which a seat raises from 0.4 to 1 m/s and a human within 2 m lowers, and takes the one that gains most on that way against how close it would bring the body within 2 s to each human and each solid, a walking human followed in a straight line at the velocity the percept carries, as MAGI follow it ([ADR 0009](adr/0009-magi-judge-a-walking-humans-course.md)). It keeps a human's rim 0.3 m outside the 0.7 m where it can, a little more where the body would pass in front of a walker rather than behind it, never steps toward a human inside that berth, and pays for straying from its last command, so it does not flip from one side of a walker or a pillar to the other. It closes on the target as the reflex does and stops on it. Like the reflex it makes no command without a goto, so under a hold it steps aside from no one. It does not close inside a standing human's 1 m even on the target, so where a person who stops or steps aside for the body stands on or beside the target, it can stop short of it for good (PLAN, Known issue 38). It is deterministic code with no model in it, the field loop blends it with the seat by the seat's sync as it blends the reflex, and the armor restrains every command it makes like any other, so every restraint below holds whatever it plans.

Either way the percept stays planar: `pose` is `x,y`, the body takes two channels, and the bridge draws the scene from above. `vel` is the velocity the body moves with, along its heading on a differential base. A differential body adds its `heading`, in radians counterclockwise from east, which the armor steers it by. The heading travels with the percept to HQ and the bridge, which draw nothing of it; the models' text leaves it out, and the recorder does not record it. A holonomic body reports a heading of 0, which the JSON leaves out, so its percepts cross the wire byte for byte as before.

A base that drives along its heading can move where the armor took motion out of the command. With a human inside 0.7 m to the north, the armor drops the command's northward part and keeps an eastward one, and a base heading 30 degrees north of east would still close on the human. So for a differential body the armor steers the restrained command through `body.steer` itself, checks the motion the base would execute against every limit again, the speed caps, the acceleration, the human and the solids within reach and the fence, and sends a turn in place instead of any motion that breaks one. Turning in place moves the body's center nowhere, so it breaks none, and the simulator moves the base with exactly the motion the armor checked until the next command. The acceleration limit bounds that motion as it bounds a holonomic body's command: while the body speeds up, its velocity changes by at most 1.5 m/s each second, and without speeding up it may change direction at once. A differential base changes direction only by turning, at most 2 rad/s, so while it turns too fast to speed up within the limit, the armor keeps it at the speed it has. A holonomic body's command gets a second check too. Speeding up from the velocity the body had can keep motion toward a human who has just come within 0.7 m, and a slide along one thing, or the fence, can bring back motion toward another, so the armor moves the body with the velocity nearest the restrained command that closes on no human within 0.7 m and no solid within reach and leaves the fence no further, which is rest where every motion within 90 degrees of the command breaks one of them. Where dropping that motion leaves the velocity further from the last one than the acceleration limit allows while the body speeds up, the armor shortens it to the most speed the limit allows in its direction, never less than the speed the body had (PLAN, Known issue 32).

Two more restraints on a differential body's command keep it moving where a turn in place would stop it again and again. The armor starts the slide along a solid or a human a little before the keep, by as far as the base closes on it while turning onto the slide, under 7 cm at 1 m/s, so the base arrives heading along the slide instead of into the solid. And it turns a slid command 5 degrees further away from what it slides along, so a base passing a walking human heads clear of where the human will be a tick later. Neither turns the command toward what it slides along, and the armor still checks what the base executes against every limit.

`BODY=mujoco` flies a differential base on MuJoCo instead of `Sim` ([ADR 0008](adr/0008-the-simulator.md)), in the same world and through the same armor. It is a cylinder of the same 0.25 m radius on planar joints, a slide along x, a slide along y and a hinge, each driven by a velocity actuator with a force limit. Each command sets the slides to the course's speed along the heading of the percept the armor checked, so the base is commanded only where the armor looked, and the hinge toward the course's heading at up to 2 rad/s. Unlike `Sim` it has mass. Its velocity follows each command with a lag of 0.05 s and brakes at no more than 10 m/s², so once the armor takes a motion out of the command, the base carries on along it by up to 2.5 cm from 0.5 m/s and 6.25 cm from 1 m/s, where `Sim` stops at once. Before that, a motion the armor lets it keep runs until the next command can take it out, one tick of the field loop and never more than the 50 ms the base steps at once, however long the tick. Every body reports how far both carry it, its stopping distance, `Sim` the one tick of the field loop it moves along a command, and the armor widens the 0.7 m around a human, the 0.35 m around a solid and the fence by it, at the fastest the body may move until the next command: about 11.7 cm at 1 m/s on the base and 2.1 cm on `Sim`. So the base brakes to a stand outside the fence, a solid's keep and the keep of a human who stands, on ticks of any length, and `Sim` stands before them a tick ahead, on the loop's 20 ms tick (PLAN, Known issue 39). A stopping distance that is negative, NaN or infinite halts the body, as a percept the armor cannot measure does. The margin cannot see a human walk into the base's braking, or step inside 0.7 m at once: on the mock missions under the reflex the base, braking along a motion the armor had already taken out, still closed on the walking human inside 0.7 m by at most 0.6 mm a tick and 2.5 mm a run. Under `PLANNER=local`, which steps away from a human inside its berth, it closed on the walker in no tick, though the walker still came up to 12 mm inside 0.7 m by its own walk. Either way the base takes a few ticks to slow to a lower speed cap (PLAN, Known issue 33). Its humans walk as `Sim`'s do and pass through the base as through `Sim`'s body: the base tells a human touching it by `Sim`'s rule and holds still while they touch, since a human that pushed it, with all the force of its scripted walk, could squeeze it into a pillar and fling it out. It zeroes its controls once the last command is 200 ms old, steps with the wall clock and pauses after a stall rather than catch up more than 50 ms at once, and an error inside MuJoCo, an unstable step among them, ends the field unit with one line that starts with `field: mujoco:` before any percept carries a bad number.
